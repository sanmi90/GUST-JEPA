\begin{abstract}
Session 32 reorganises the project around a predict--correct state--estimation thesis on a
\emph{pooled} $d{=}32$ coefficient latent (the estimable object), with the $32{\times}24{\times}12$
spatial latent kept as a separate decodability tier. On the frozen v2.2 split (102 cases /
450 encounters, symmetric $|G|{=}4$ Test~C) we (i) resolved two pre--launch blockers with
direct physical/gradient checks, (ii) trained the full pooled canonical matrix and passed the
pre--registered gates P1 (readability), P2 (merit ordering), P3 (near--null departure
mechanism), with P4 (pooling cost) tabulated, (iii) built a leakage--free ensemble Kalman
filter that senses wall pressure at model--conditioned optimal taps, and (iv) characterised the
\emph{operating envelope}: the gust intensity $|G|$ and diameter $D$ beyond which each part of
the pipeline stops being able to do anything. The central new result is that the sequential
filter tracks lift ($C_L$ analysis $R^2 = 0.71$--$0.90$ for $|G|\ge 1$) and \emph{extends the
usable range beyond static single--frame recovery}, which fails by $|G|\approx 3$; this is the
direct successor to the manuscript's lead--time pressure$\to C_L$ result ($R^2 = 0.35$). We are
honest about the soft spot: the filter's uncertainty is mis--calibrated at strong/wide gusts,
and the operative attribution claim is C2 (readability is supervision--driven), not C1.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{One--paragraph story.} On the pooled tier, readability and matched--predictor merit are
\emph{supervision--driven}: an encoder with only the lift and wake heads (no predictive
objective, no anti--collapse) matches the full predictive JEPA. The predictive objective earns
its place elsewhere --- P3 shows it (with anti--collapse) keeps rollout departures inside the
training distribution while bare reconstruction leaks into near--null directions. Wall
observability is real but limited: the estimability margin is weak, the wall sees the
force/enstrophy latent directions and is blind to wake circulation, and the sequential filter
improves lift tracking over open--loop and over static recovery but with mis--calibrated
uncertainty at the strongest gusts. The operating envelope makes this precise.

\section{Setup and pre--launch blockers}

\textbf{Data.} Split v2.2, 102 cases / 450 encounters (84 train / 10 test\_b / 8 test\_c /
val 100), with a \emph{symmetric} $|G|{=}4$ Test~C (4 periodic $+$ 4 run4). Two blockers were
cleared with direct evidence rather than assumption:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{B1 (run4 gust sign).} The archive uses the solver convention $s=-G$, and the
parser sets $G_{\text{inv}}=s$, so split $G$ is the negation of physical $G$. A non--circular
lift--sign check across all 8 Test~C cases confirmed run4 is physically consistent with periodic:
every periodic $G{+}4$ dips negative first ($C_L$ trough $\approx -7.6$), every run4 $G{-}4$
rises positive first (peak $\approx +8.3$) --- near mirror images. Test~C symmetry and the
sign--asymmetry analyses are safe.
\item \textbf{B2 (AE$+$pooled decoder).} The pooled reconstruction path exists and trains: the
parameter--free adapter feeds a trainable $6.3$M--param decoder; all 90 decoder tensors receive
nonzero gradient from the reconstruction loss, and an 80--iter real--data burst dropped
reconstruction loss $0.193\to 0.105$ ($-45.6\%$).
\end{itemize}

each model's own latent) instead of one shared target--blind qDEIM array, and the
sensor$\to$latent recovery was upgraded from kernel ridge to a CV--selected choice of

\textbf{Gate O stays \weak} ($\Delta R^2 = +0.120$, below $0.2$), but the honest finding sharpens:

reconstruction latent is most recoverable in variance ($0.921$) yet carries the least
wall--recoverable physics ($0.470$), while the predictive latent carries the most ($0.637$). The
wall--visibility spectrum (O2) shows the wall sees the force ($C_L,C_D$) and wake--enstrophy latent
directions and is blind to the near--null wake--circulation direction.

\section{Track B: the predict--correct filter}

A stochastic ensemble Kalman filter ($N{=}64$, deterministic square--root variant available)
advances the frozen pooled state through the matched AR--transformer and corrects with
$K{=}8$ span--averaged wall--pressure taps. The observation operator $H\!:\,z\to p_K$ is
\emph{pressure only}; the physics observables ($E_w, C_L$) are eval--time readouts and can never
reach the innovation (a leakage guarantee enforced by construction and by unit test).

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
\item Verified: 10/10 unit tests (synthetic linear--Gaussian tracking, covariance positive
definite, EnKF $\to$ exact Kalman within Monte--Carlo tolerance).
\item Model--conditioned OSP taps improve $C_L$ closure over the shared qDEIM array by $-36\%$
RMSE (jepa\_pool impact).
\item Tuning frozen on test\_a only: $\rho{=}1.0$ (the filter is \emph{under}--dispersed, mean
NIS $\approx 5 < K{=}8$, so more inflation hurts). Warning thresholds $\epsilon_A=\epsilon_t=0.25$.
\item \textbf{Honest caveat.} Innovations are not white ($|$lag--1$|\approx 0.88$; wall pressure
is temporally smooth), and the strongest gusts drive the filter to statistical divergence. The
filter improves closure over open--loop but is not yet well calibrated --- S33 needs a genuine
process/observation noise ($Q,R$) model, not just inflation.
\end{itemize}

\gd{* weak--signal regime: true lift near--constant, $R^2$ denominator $\approx 0$; not a failure.}

\textbf{Four reads.}
(1) Filter lift tracking is good for \emph{all} $|G|\ge 1$ ($R^2\,0.69$--$0.90$), even at $|G|{=}4$ ---
not a middle band.
(2) What degrades monotonically with $|G|$ is \emph{calibration}, not tracking: divergence
$0.00\to 0.82$, mean NIS $0.9\to 19.4$; divergence exceeds $50\%$ first at $|G|\approx 3$
($D\le 1.0$) / $|G|\approx 2$ ($D{=}1.5$). ``Divergence'' $\ne$ ``useless''.
(3) \textbf{The sequential filter extends the usable envelope beyond static recovery} --- the
paper's core argument, quantified: static recovery goes negative at $|G|{=}3$/$4$ while the
filter still tracks ($0.69$/$0.90$). Open--loop forecast never works, so the correction step is
essential.
(4) \textbf{Strong sign asymmetry} (representation--general; POD replicates): physical--positive
gusts have a wider envelope --- divergence $0.16$ vs $0.35$, filter $C_L$ median $0.89$ vs
$0.25$. The $|G|{=}3$ limit is in--distribution, so it is an observability/filter limit, not a
training--coverage gap.

\section{H\_roll ablation (multi--step rollout on v2.2)}
The manuscript never had a v2.2 test of whether the multi--step--rollout training objective
($H_{\text{roll}}{=}8$, the frozen kit default) actually helps. We trained \texttt{jepa\_nowake\_pool}
at $H_{\text{roll}}{=}1$ (via a non--destructive, default--off \texttt{--horizon-override} flag)
and compared it against the existing $H_{\text{roll}}{=}8$ model, evaluating both with fresh/matched
downstream operators on frozen latents (isolating the encoder--geometry effect of the objective).

\textbf{Verdict: multi--step rollout DOES help on v2.2 pooled}, and the advantage grows with
forecast horizon.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
axis (Test~B, $h8$; lower VRMSE/drift better) & $H_{\text{roll}}{=}1$ & $H_{\text{roll}}{=}8$ & $\Delta(8{-}1)$ \\
\midrule
observable merit (mean 5--obs MLP $R^2$) & 0.407 & 0.471 & $\mathbf{+0.064}$ \\
$C_L$ forecast closure (MLP $R^2$)        & 0.429 & 0.691 & $\mathbf{+0.262}$ \\
wake--enstrophy closure                   & 0.284 & 0.305 & $+0.021$ \\
field VRMSE                               & 1.006 & 0.983 & $-0.024$ \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

The mechanism is transparent in the merit--vs--horizon curve: the two are tied at short horizons
($h1\approx 0.54$ both), but by $h16$ the single--step latent \emph{collapses to $0.046$} while the
multi--step latent holds $0.312$. Single--step training yields a latent that forecasts fine one
step out but falls off its manifold under rollout; the multi--step objective buys long--horizon
rollout stability --- the same on--manifold argument the paper makes for JEPA vs reconstruction
(\S2 / \S1b), now shown for the rollout \emph{horizon} itself. The frozen--filter axis (3 test\_a
cases) leans the same way ($C_L$ analysis $R^2$ $0.395\to 0.551$ with per--model OSP) but is noisy
and does not carry the verdict. Caveat: this measures $H_{\text{roll}}$'s effect on the
\emph{learned representation} (matched predictors on frozen latents); the native co--trained
predictors would likely show an even larger gap.

\section{O2 fixed--$\delta$ normalisation variant}
We re--ran the wall--visibility spectrum with a single fixed absolute perturbation
$\delta = \sqrt{\overline{\mathrm{Var}_j}} = 0.991$ applied identically to every unit PC (raw
energy $\sum_t \|h(z_t)-h(\tilde z_t)\|^2$, no $1/\text{std}^2$ factor), to test whether the
raw ranking's near--null dominance was a normalisation artifact.

\textbf{It is not.} The fixed--$\delta$ raw ranking is essentially identical to the
std--normalised one: the two energy arrays correlate $0.9928$ across all 32 directions, and
$\mathrm{corr}(\text{evr},\text{energy}) = -0.514$ (fixed) vs $-0.515$ (std). Top--visible is
still the near--null dir~20 (evr $0.005$, $C_D$); top--hidden is the highest--variance dir~0
(evr $0.19$, circulation--neg). So the near--null ``visibility'' is a \emph{genuine property of
the estimation--tier AR--transformer}: it has large per--unit gain along the low--variance
directions it never learned to damp (they were never excited in training, so its dynamics there
are data--unconstrained), and those project onto the pressure head. The std--normalised and
fixed--$\delta$ metrics rank the same because the perturbations sit in the linear regime.

The robust, defensible statement is therefore the meaningful--direction ranking (evr $\ge 0.02$,
$89\%$ of variance), which holds under \emph{both} metrics: the wall sees the force ($C_L,C_D$)
and wake--enstrophy modes (dirs 12, 9, 4, 3) and is blind to the highest--variance
wake--circulation mode (dir~0). \textbf{Filter--relevant corollary:} the transformer's large gain
along data--unconstrained near--null directions is a plausible noise--injection mechanism during
assimilation --- a concrete candidate cause of the Track~B calibration/divergence issues and the
envelope's calibration ceiling, linking O2 to the filter's honest soft spot.
