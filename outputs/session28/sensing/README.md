# Sparse wall-pressure observability (Track C-F, v2.1) -- MAIN result

Generated by `scripts/session28/sensing_cf.py` on the v2.1 unconditioned rebuild
(split `configs/splits/split_v2p1.json`, cache `v2p1`, DNS metrics
`outputs/session28/exp2/dns_physical_metrics.npz`). CPU-only sklearn; one full
run is ~3 min wall. Closes referees B5, M7, M11, M12 (master plan C-F, D195).

This is the deferred but now-ELEVATED observability track: it asks whether the
predictive latent that makes the wake linearly readable from held-out fields
(the closure matrix) is also the state most recoverable from a handful of wall
taps. The answer on v2.1 is nuanced and is stated honestly below; nothing here
is overclaimed.

## Pipeline (what is wired)

For each family (jepa_tf_noc, fukami, pod, bvae) at d = 64, lead seed:

1. Read a causal wall-pressure window of `W = 30` frames ENDING at the readout
   frame `readout = impact + H`, `H = 16` (the closure-matrix primary endpoint),
   over `K in {2, 4, 8, 16}` taps placed by qDEIM (primary) or per-family TCSI
   (secondary). The window is `p_wall[readout - W : readout]`, so the pressure
   is available up to (and not beyond) the readout instant.
2. Fit `KernelRidge(RBF, alpha = 0.1, gamma = 0.01)` mapping the flattened
   `(K * W)` pressure vector to the readout latent `z(readout)` (the established
   pressure -> latent map, ported from `session27_exp_pressure_v2_tcsi_uncond.py`).
3. Apply the FROZEN closure-matrix wake probe: a ridge fitted ONCE on the
   family's TRAIN per-frame latents to predict `wake_enstrophy`, never refit on
   pressure. This is the "fixed linear probe" of the deployment panel.
4. Report state recovery, wake-via-latent, lift-via-latent, and pressure -> Y,
   with direct (no-latent) pressure -> observable regressions as baselines.

Probe regime: pressure -> latent is a STATE-RECOVERY map (declared in the script
docstring); the wake probe is the frozen state-descriptor closure probe.

## Metrics (defined by equation; both reported so anisotropy is visible)

Variance-weighted state-recovery R^2 (pooled over the d latent dimensions):

    R2_vw = 1 - sum_j SSE_j / sum_j SST_j,
    SSE_j = sum_i (zhat_ij - z_ij)^2,
    SST_j = sum_i (z_ij - mean_i z_ij)^2,

with SST about the held-out split's own per-dimension mean. High-variance
directions dominate this scalar, so it can hide anisotropy.

Mean canonical correlation over the first `n_dir = min(d, K * W)` directions:

    rho_1 >= ... >= rho_r = singular values of
        M = Sigma_pp^{-1/2} Sigma_pt Sigma_tt^{-1/2},
    CC = mean(rho_1, ..., rho_{n_dir}),

between predicted and true held-out latents. Reporting `R2_vw` ALONGSIDE `CC`
exposes whether the recovery is isotropic (many large rho) or concentrated in a
few high-variance directions (high `R2_vw`, low `CC`).

## Placement (i): qDEIM is the PRIMARY cross-family placement

qDEIM (Drmac & Gugercin 2016; Manohar et al. 2018) is target-blind: it pivots
the leading right singular vectors of the TRAIN window-mean pressure snapshot, a
matrix that is identical for every family, so the qDEIM picks are
family-independent. This makes it the fair cross-family placement. Per-family
TCSI (target-aware greedy on the latent's first principal component) is reported
as the secondary placement; its picks differ per family and are in
`sensor_picks_v2p1.json`.

qDEIM picks (physical taps 0-191): K=2 [5,11]; K=4 [9,11,13,107]; K=8
[10,11,12,17,48,106,147,187]; K=16 [3,9,10,11,12,15,21,31,49,76,96,107,111,140,
166,183]. The early taps cluster near the leading-edge suction peak (taps ~10-12).

## Results (test_b, K = 8, qDEIM, d = 64 lead seed)

State recovery (the result that HOLDS): the predictive latent is the most
recoverable from sparse wall pressure.

| family       | state R2_vw | mean CC | interpretation                          |
|--------------|-------------|---------|-----------------------------------------|
| jepa_tf_noc  | +0.78       | 0.56    | high recovery, relatively isotropic     |
| fukami       | +0.66       | 0.15    | high R2_vw but only a few directions    |
| bvae (match) | +0.51       | 0.13    | as fukami                               |
| pod          | +0.34       | 0.64    | low R2_vw, many directions (inverted)   |

The jepa_tf_noc state recovery leads all families at every K (0.70/0.76/0.78/
0.71 at K=2/4/8/16). The variance-weighted and canonical-correlation columns
disagree in their ordering (POD has the highest CC but the lowest R2_vw), which
is exactly the anisotropy the dual metric is meant to surface and the reason a
single scalar is not reported.

Deployment panel (pressure -> z(impact+16) -> frozen wake probe -> wake
enstrophy), with case-clustered 95% CI:

| family       | pressure -> wake R2 | 95% CI (case-clustered) |
|--------------|---------------------|-------------------------|
| jepa_tf_noc  | +0.18               | [-1.19, +0.64]          |
| bvae (match) | +0.13               | [-2.16, +0.56]          |
| fukami       | -0.00               | [-4.58, +0.11]          |
| pod          | -0.51               | [-15.26, -0.05]         |
| direct (no latent) | +0.17         | -                       |

HONEST SCOPING (this is a MAIN result, so the limitation is stated, not buried):
the predictive latent gives the best pressure -> wake R2 of any family and is
the only family with a positive value across all K, and per-encounter it beats
fukami (sign test p = 0.022) and POD (p < 0.001, case-clustered CI on the error
reduction excludes zero) at K = 8. BUT the JEPA pressure -> wake R2 (+0.18) only
marginally exceeds the direct no-latent baseline (+0.17), and the JEPA-vs-bvae
difference is not significant (sign test p = 0.14). Sparse wall pressure carries
limited information about the post-impact wake enstrophy at this configuration:
the high state-recovery R2 lives in high-variance latent directions that wall
pressure observes well, while the wake-bearing directions (the ones the frozen
probe reads) are recovered only weakly (the JEPA latent-to-wake-direction
correlation is ~0.39). The deployment claim is therefore scoped to STATE
RECOVERY -- "the predictive latent is the most recoverable state from sparse
wall pressure" -- and NOT to a strong pressure -> wake forecast. The lift panel
behaves the same way: pressure -> z -> C_L gives R2 = 0.43 for JEPA, matching
the direct C_L baseline (0.44), so the latent route neither helps nor hurts C_L.

Extrapolation (test_c, |G| = 4): every family's state recovery and wake-via-
latent go negative (jepa state R2 = -1.36), i.e. the K-tap pressure map does not
generalise to the |G| = 4 envelope. This mirrors the closure-matrix and
lead-time test_c behaviour and is reported, not hidden.

## Causality (v): one sentence, plus the measured delta

The omega-pipeline per-encounter percentile clip uses within-encounter
statistics, so to bound any leakage we re-ran the JEPA headline pressure -> wake
under a causal-clip variant that winsorises each tap to the TRAIN causal-window
[p0.1, p99.9] (global TRAIN statistics, never within-encounter). The delta is
+0.0009 (base +0.183 -> causal +0.184), i.e. negligible, as expected: the
pressure window here is read directly from the cache and is not itself
percentile-clipped.

## Y attribution (vi): the M11 softening

Pressure -> Y R2 = 0.51 (K = 8, qDEIM, test_b). The wall-normal gust offset Y is
recoverable from a few wall taps, which softens the Y limitation: the limitation
in the manuscript is the post-impact single-frame mid-plane vorticity observable
and the Y sampling density, NOT "the data" -- Y leaves a clear, sparsely
observable wall-pressure signature.

## Statistics

- 5-fold CASE-level CV on every readout (`make_case_folds`, ported from the
  closure matrix; no case straddles folds).
- Case-clustered bootstrap 95% CI on the headline pressure -> wake R2 per family
  (resample CASES with replacement, 2000 draws).
- Paired family differences use a case-clustered CI + one-sided sign test
  (`stats_lib.sign_test_one_sided` + `stats_lib.case_cluster_bootstrap`), NOT
  `stats_lib.case_permutation_p` (degenerate for paired location tests; the B6
  lesson).
