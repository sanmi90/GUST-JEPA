\subsubsection{The shared \DirectFC}
\label{sec:methods_rex}

\rb{K}{S33-01}{Defines the one operator under which every family is compared. The forecast result and the filter's process noise both depend on this specification.}The forecast-merit comparison of \S\ref{sec:res_forecast} requires one
operator trained identically on every family's latents; the operator used
throughout is the \DirectFC, a direct sequence-to-sequence realisation of
equation~\eqref{eq:forecast} that emits the full horizon in one pass. It is a
quantile forecaster whose design
transfers the deployable ingredients of the TiRex forecasting line \citep{tirex,tirex2} without its foundation-model machinery. The operator reads a lookback
window of the $L$ states preceding the forecast. $L$ is randomised during
training, drawn uniformly from $16$ to $30$ so that the operator is not tuned to
one context length, and held fixed at $L = 25$ for every evaluation reported
here. Each window is normalised on itself,
$\tilde{\zvec} = \operatorname{arcsinh}\!\left((\zvec - \mu)/\sigma\right)$
with $\mu, \sigma$ the window mean and standard deviation (floored at
$10^{-3}$), passed through a two-layer LSTM of width $512$, and mapped by
a single feed-forward head to the full forecast tensor of $40$ horizon
steps, $d$ coordinates and nine quantiles $q \in \{0.1, \dots, 0.9\}$ in
one shot; the inverse transform restores physical scale. There is no
autoregressive feedback, so rollout error cannot compound by construction. That
is the reason for the design rather than a side effect of it: forecasts of
learned latent coordinates in actuated wake flows degrade with horizon and can
diverge catastrophically \citep{solerarico_compactness_underreview}, and
emitting the whole horizon at once removes error compounding as one route to
that failure.
Training minimises the pinball loss of quantile regression \citep{koenker1978}
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}|} \sum_{q \in \mathcal{Q}}
  \left\langle \rho_q\!\left(z - \hat{z}_q\right) \right\rangle,
  \qquad
  \rho_q(u) = \max\!\left(q u,\ (q - 1) u\right),
  \label{eq:pinball}
\end{equation}
with AdamW. The loss is used because the filter needs a spread and not only a
point estimate: $\rho_q$ is minimised in expectation by the $q$th quantile of the
predictive distribution, so the nine outputs are calibrated quantiles rather than
an arbitrary band, and the width between them can be read directly as a forecast
uncertainty. The median forecast is the point prediction everywhere; that
inter-quantile width supplies the state-dependent process noise of
\S\ref{sec:methods_rexenkf}. The optimiser settings, the one-off backbone and
width selection over two widths and two quantile counts, and the adopted and rejected
ingredients of the TiRex source line, including the oracle-covariate negative of
\S\ref{sec:res_forecast}, are catalogued in the supplementary material.\re
