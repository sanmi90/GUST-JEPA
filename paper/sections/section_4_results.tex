We report the controlled representational comparisons (\S\ref{sec:res_carries} to
\S\ref{sec:res_wall}) on the in-distribution test\_b encounters and hold the
out-of-distribution test\_c set in reserve, so that the $|G| = 4$ extrapolation

estimator, the recovery, and the operating envelope

in Appendix~\ref{app:reg}. Every quantity quoted below is a held-out value at the
readout horizon unless stated otherwise.

\subsection{What the coefficient state carries, and what supplies it}
\label{sec:res_carries}
\label{sec:res_closure} % v2.1 alias

The comparison between reduced states is meaningful only if the wake is a
discriminating endpoint, and it is. At the readout horizon the release parameters
no longer fix the wake enstrophy, while they still fix the forces, so the wake is
where a representation that has captured the gust-vortex physics parts company
from one that has captured only its force signature. Table~\ref{tab:closure} reports
what each pooled coefficient state carries, read by a fixed linear probe from a
held-out test\_b field with no rollout. The wake is read cleanly by every state that
carries the wake-supervision head, with a held-out $R^2$ between $\XwakeAeWake$ and
$\XwakeSupOnly$, and it collapses for every state that does not, falling to
$\XwakeJepaNowake$ for the predictive latent trained without the head. Instantaneous
readability of the wake is therefore supplied by the observable supervision, not by
any training objective.

The objective-free supervised encoder makes this concrete. Trained with the wake
head but no predictive or reconstructive objective at all, it reads the wake at
least as well as the full predictive latent ($\XwakeSupOnly$ against $\XwakeJepaWake$)
and ties it on the merit a matched predictor extracts by forecast
($\XmeritSupOnly$ against $\XmeritJepaWake$). On this tier, then, we do not
attribute the readability or the fitted-predictor merit to the predictive
objective over the supervised control: the paired readability gap is
$\PoneWakeDelta$ in the wake with a confidence interval
$[\PoneWakeDeltalo, \PoneWakeDeltahi]$ that includes zero, and the merit ordering
places the two supervised states together and above the reconstructive and
anti-collapse recipes ($\XmeritJepaWake \ge \XmeritSupOnly > \XmeritAeWake >
\XmeritRegAE$). The gap between the predictive state and the matched
reconstructive control survives independent retraining, at
$\SeedMeritJepaWake \pm \SeedMeritSdJepaWake$ against
$\SeedMeritAeWake \pm \SeedMeritSdAeWake$ over three seeds each, whereas the
objective-free control's merit does not (its three seeds spread by
$\SeedMeritSdSupOnly$), a reliability point we return to in
\S\ref{sec:disc_limits}. We state the representational result as the supervision's, and turn
to what the predictive objective does add, which is dynamical.

One inversion in the table fixes the coordinate-level reading. The predictive
latent trained without the wake head carries the highest single-observable lift
forecast of any state ($\XclJepaNowake$) yet the lowest wake readability
($\XwakeJepaNowake$), because the forces are held redundantly across the latent
while the wake is not, the distributed-code signature we return to in
\S\ref{sec:res_latent_physics}. Among the reference recipes the lift-augmented
reconstructive autoencoder reads the wake only when it carries the wake head
($\XwakeFukamiWake$ against $\XwakeFukami$ without it), and the linear basis does
not reach the wake at all ($\XwakePod$), so no linear projection recovers the
enstrophy that a supervised nonlinear state does.

\subsection{Why the state stays usable under rollout}
\label{sec:res_rollout}
\label{sec:res_controls} % v2.1 alias
\label{sec:res_horizon} % v2.1 alias
\label{sec:res_drift} % v2.1 alias

A state that reads the wake at a single instant need not survive a rollout, because
a probe attached to a drifted latent is queried outside its support. We ask where
each rolled state goes relative to the training distribution by decomposing its
departure onto the principal and the near-null directions of the encoded covariance
(table~\ref{tab:mechanism}). The reconstruction carrying only an anti-collapse term
is near-isotropic, its covariance condition number close to unity ($\YcondRegAE$),
and its rollout leaks a tenth of its departure into the near-null directions
($\YnearnullRegAE$). The supervised states, predictive and objective-free alike,
are strongly anisotropic, their variance concentrated in a low-rank subspace
(condition numbers $\YcondJepaWake$ and $\YcondSupOnly$), and their rollouts keep
their departures inside it ($\YnearnullJepaWake$ and $\YnearnullSupOnly$, against
the $\YisoNull$ of an isotropic null). Supervision, not the anti-collapse term
alone, builds the protected subspace: it pins the encoded variance onto the
observable-aligned directions, and the forward model, having only ever seen states
there, moves within it. The anti-collapse term on its own drives the latent toward
isotropy, as an independent adaptation of the same regulariser to biosignals also
reports \citep{broustail2026}, and it is the supervision that supplies the geometry
the rollout needs.

What the predictive objective adds is long-horizon stability, and it is the
multi-step form of the objective that adds it. Trained with a single-step rather
than a multi-step rollout, the same predictive latent is tied one step out and then
separates under rollout: by sixteen steps the single-step latent's forecast merit
has fallen to $\ZmeritSixteenHone$ while the multi-step latent holds
$\ZmeritSixteenHeight$, its lift forecast closure is higher by
$\ZclHeight$ against $\ZclHone$, and its on-manifold drift is lower
($\ZdriftHeight$ against $\ZdriftHone$ at horizon sixteen). The objective-free
supervised state, which has the readability and the protected subspace but no
predictive training, is the weakest field forecaster of the supervised group. The
contribution of the predictive objective is therefore forward rather than
instantaneous: it keeps the rolled state both in distribution and near the true
trajectory, which is exactly what a sequential estimator queries.

\subsection{What the wall can see}
\label{sec:res_wall}
\label{sec:res_observability} % v2.1 alias

At deployment the state must be read from the wall, so we ask how much of each
latent a sparse set of pressure taps recovers over a window ending at the readout
instant (table~\ref{tab:recovery}). Two findings organise the answer. First,
raw-variance recoverability and physics recoverability disagree: the reconstruction
latent is the most recoverable in state variance ($\WstateFukami$) yet carries the
least wall-recoverable physics ($\WobsFukami$ in observable closure), while the
predictive latent carries the most physics ($\WobsJepa$) from a less recoverable
state ($\WstateJepa \pm \WstateJepaSd$ over three seeds). The wall aligns with many of the reconstruction latent's
directions, but they are the low-variance ones that do not carry the observables;
it aligns with fewer of the predictive latent's directions, but those are the ones
that hold the state. Second, perturbing the predictive state along its principal
directions and measuring the signal that reaches the pressure head shows the wall
reading the force and wake-enstrophy directions and blind to the highest-variance
wake-circulation direction, a ranking unchanged under a fixed-amplitude
perturbation and so a property of the learned dynamics rather than of the
normalisation. The wall, in short, reads the force well and the wake circulation
not at all, which is why the state must be estimated sequentially rather than
inverted from one frame.

The margin of the coefficient state over a raw high-dimensional field latent is
real but modest. Its recoverability advantage is $\GateODelta$ in $R^2$, with a
confidence interval $[\GateODeltalo, \GateODeltahi]$ that clears zero but not the
pre-registered strong-effect bar of $0.2$. We therefore do not claim a strong-form
estimability gate; the defensible statement is the clean physics-versus-variance
ordering above.

\subsubsection{Sensors traded for delays}
\label{sec:res_delays}

That the wall is blind to the wake circulation at a single instant is a statement
about one frame, not about the sensor count, and the delay-embedding reading of
\S\ref{sec:methods_estimator} makes a testable prediction: the circulation the
instantaneous pressure misses should become recoverable as the pressure is read
over a longer window. It does. Holding the tap count at eight and lengthening the
window, the recovery of the wall-blind wake circulation rises from near its
single-frame value to $\ToneCircNegWThirty$ at a thirty-frame window, a gain of
$\ToneCircDelta$ with a case-clustered interval $[\ToneCircDeltalo,
\ToneCircDeltahi]$ that excludes zero, while the force, already visible at one
instant, barely moves. The delays recover the coordinate the sensors cannot.

Varying the tap count and the window together traces a spatial-for-temporal trade
(figure~\ref{fig:trade}). A single tap read over a thirty-frame window recovers the
coefficient state as well as eight taps read at one instant, and intermediate
combinations of tap count and window meet in between, because the encounter is
low-dimensional and a state that filled its ambient dimension would not admit the
trade. The eight-tap, thirty-frame recovery reconciles exactly with the static
recovery above ($\TbridgeState$), and the delay stride sits at the cache cadence,
close to the first mutual-information minimum of the tap signals at
$\TmiTau$ frames.

The grid uses a shared target-blind tap array so that the trade is free of any
model-conditioned placement; rerunning its eight-tap, thirty-frame cell on the
model-conditioned taps of table~\ref{tab:recovery} reproduces that table's
recovery exactly ($\TbridgeState$ against $\WstateJepa$), so the two analyses
meet where their sensing coincides. This trade belongs to the static delay-coordinate
reconstruction, and it does not transfer to the frozen sequential filter, which
needs its eight taps. Rerunning the filter with only its own leading taps, and
changing nothing else, drives the analysis lift below the skill of the
encounter mean at every gust strength the eight-tap filter tracks (at
$|G| = 1$ the single-tap analysis loses $22.5$ in median paired $R^2$ against
the eight-tap filter, interval $[\TtwobKOneDeltaGOnelo,
\TtwobKOneDeltaGOnehi]$). The rank of the innovation, with the process and
observation noise held at their calibrated values, is what the filter cannot
spare: its sensor budget is a calibration constraint, not a delay-embedding
one, which points at the noise model of \S\ref{sec:disc_limits} rather than at
fewer sensors.

\subsection{Tracking the encounter from the wall}
\label{sec:res_tracking}

We now run the estimator. It is initialised from wall pressure alone before impact
and corrected at every frame through the encounter, and it never sees the vorticity
field. Figure~\ref{fig:hero} follows representative held-out encounters, the true
lift and wake enstrophy against the open-loop rollout with no correction, the
sliding-window pressure-only regression with no dynamics, and the filter analysis.
The open-loop rollout tracks the pre-impact baseline and then loses the encounter,
as a forecast with no measurement must; the pressure-only regression follows the
force while its window is representative and goes stale through the transient; the
filter follows the load through impact and into relaxation. This realises, on the
wake-supervised coefficient state, the online-estimation pathway the
extreme-aerodynamic control literature has named but not closed
\citep{fukami2024control,mousavi2025,eldredge2025guide}: the state a real-time
controller would need is recovered from sparse wall pressure and advanced by the
learned dynamics, without ever measuring the field.

Two honest boundaries frame what this demonstration does and does not establish,
and we state them before the envelope. First, tracking the load inside the
training range is not unique to this state. Running the identical frozen filter
on every family (table~\ref{tab:family_filter}), the nonlinear latents all track
the lift for $|G|$ between one and two, and the wake-supervised reconstructive
control is the best of them at the mildest gusts ($\FfCLAeWakeGOne$ at
$|G| = 1$ against $\VfiltCLGOne$ for the predictive state); only the linear
basis fails outright. What no static approach achieves is the strong-gust range:
a windowed regression from the same taps straight to the lift, with no latent at
all, fades to $\DirectCLKeightGThree$ by a gust ratio of three, and even the
full wall array recovers only $\DirectCLAllTapsGFour$ at the boundary, so the
sequential accumulation of pressure through a learned nonlinear state, rather
than any particular training objective, is what carries the load into the
strong-gust regime. Where the objectives separate is at that boundary and in the
filter's statistical health: the predictive state alone combines the highest
boundary closure ($\VfiltCLGFour$ against $\FfCLAeWakeGFour$ to
$\FfCLFukamiWakeGFour$ for the alternatives), the lowest divergence rate at
every gust strength, and the least-degraded wake readout. Second, no family's
filter tracks the wake enstrophy: the analysis wake closure is below the mean
predictor for every state, least badly for the predictive one, so the wake
content of the encounter is established at the representational tier
(\S\ref{sec:res_carries}) and is not yet recoverable through the assimilation.
The filter is a load estimator whose state carries wake information; it is not a
wake estimator, and we say so plainly.

\subsection{The gust-intensity operating envelope}
\label{sec:res_envelope}

A reduced state earns its place at deployment only if it can be followed from the
sensors that are present, and the wall pressure a small vehicle can carry does not,
at a single instant, fix the wake it leaves behind (\S\ref{sec:res_wall}). The
question is therefore not whether the state is readable but how far into the gust an
estimator that sees only the wall can track it. The sequential filter reaches much
further than the single-frame inverse. While the encounter stays mild, the static
single-frame recovery of the lift holds, with a held-out $R^2$ of $\VrecCLGOne$ at
$|G| = 1$ and $\VrecCLGOneHalf$ at $|G| = 1.5$; as the gust strengthens it
collapses, falling below the skill of predicting the encounter mean by a gust ratio
of three ($\VrecCLGThree$) and remaining there at the $|G| = 4$ boundary
($\VrecCLGFour$), because a single pressure frame cannot see the reorganised wake a
strong gust deposits. Reading the same taps through the learned dynamics, the filter
still follows the load across those encounters, from $\VfiltCLGOne$ at $|G| = 1$ to
$\VfiltCLGThree$ at a ratio of three and $\VfiltCLGFour$ at the boundary
(table~\ref{tab:envelope}, figure~\ref{fig:envelope}). The open-loop forecast never
tracks the load at any gust strength, so it is the correction step, not the forward
model, that recovers the state, and it does so from pressure the visibility analysis
found blind to the wake circulation: the sequential dynamics carry the part of the
state the wall cannot see at an instant.

\input{sections/tables/table_envelope.tex}

What weakens with gust strength is not the tracking but the filter's own account of
its uncertainty. The divergence rate climbs from none at $|G| = 1$ to four
encounters in five at the boundary, and the mean normalised innovation squared from
near unity in the undisturbed limit ($\VnisGZero$) to nearly twenty at $|G| = 4$
($\VnisGFour$), crossing the half-divergence mark near a ratio of $\VdivThreshDOne$
for the compact cores and near $\VdivThreshDOneHalf$ for the widest. The ensemble
collapses while the analysis mean still follows the load, so the filter is
under-dispersed rather than mistracking. We read this as the signature of a missing
noise model: the innovations are correlated in time rather than white, and the
remedy is a calibrated process and observation covariance rather than the covariance
inflation we deliberately withheld (\S\ref{sec:disc_limits}).

The envelope is not symmetric in the sign of the gust, and the asymmetry survives in
the linear basis, so it belongs to the flow rather than to the representation
(figure~\ref{fig:envelope}(b)). The physically positive gusts, which roll up the
larger and longer-lived leading-edge vortex (\S\ref{sec:res_latent_physics}), leave
the wider usable envelope. Sampling the $|G| = 4$ boundary at both signs
(\S\ref{sec:flow_observables}) is what lets this asymmetry be measured rather than
assumed.

The boundary sits at a gust ratio of three, inside the training range, so it marks a
limit of what the mid-plane pressure can observe and the filter assimilate, not a
gap in training coverage. Read through delay coordinates the two limits are one. The
filter follows the load where the single frame fails because it reconstructs the
state from a window of pressure rather than an instant
(\S\ref{sec:methods_estimator}), and it loses its calibration where the strengthening
gust drives the interaction three-dimensional and the encounter's effective
dimension outgrows what a fixed tap count and delay window can embed
(\S\ref{sec:res_wall}). Only at $|G| = 4$ does the mid-plane representation meet the
genuine three-dimensional observability boundary the field itself reaches
(\S\ref{sec:flow_physics}), now seen from the filter. The envelope is therefore two
claims with different owners: its extension beyond every static inverse belongs to
the sequential estimator on a nonlinear latent, a property the family comparison of
\S\ref{sec:res_tracking} shows is shared; its width at the strongest gusts, its
calibration margin, and the wake content of the state being tracked belong to the
wake-supervised predictive state. This envelope, not the single-instant
readability, is what a wall-estimable reduced state is for.

\subsection{The physics the latent state holds}
\label{sec:res_latent_physics}
\label{sec:res_flow_physics} % v2.1 alias
\label{sec:res_code} % v2.1 alias
\label{sec:res_param_phase} % v2.1 alias
\label{sec:res_physical} % v2.1 alias

The estimation results rest on a representation, and this subsection reads what the
representation holds in measures that do not depend on the filter. The predictive
latent carries the shedding clock. Fitting a linear operator to the encoded
limit-cycle trajectory by dynamic mode decomposition places a marginally stable
oscillatory mode at Strouhal number $\DmdStJepa$, against the measured shedding line,
with modulus $\DmdLamJepa$ just below unity. The Fukami-lineage autoencoders lose
that clock: their leading latent mode is heavily damped and off-frequency
($\DmdStFukami$ at modulus $\DmdLamFukami$ without the wake head, $\DmdStFukamiWake$
at $\DmdLamFukamiWake$ with it). The structured states keep it, whether their
structure comes from prediction, from supervision, from anti-collapse, or from a
linear basis: the objective-free supervised latent, the anti-collapse
reconstruction, and the proper orthogonal basis all place the shedding mode near the
measured frequency on a near-unit-modulus orbit ($\DmdStSupOnly$, $\DmdStRegAE$ and
$\DmdStPod$ at moduli $\DmdLamSupOnly$, $\DmdLamRegAE$ and $\DmdLamPod$). It is the
Fukami-lineage reconstruction, not reconstruction in general, that fails to hold the
shedding spectrum.

The wake is a collective code rather than a labelled coordinate. The gap between the
wake readability of the full predictive latent and that of its single most
informative coordinate is $\WcGapJepa$, and $\WcGapSupOnly$ for the objective-free
supervised state, against $\WcGapFukamiWake$ and $\WcGapPod$ for the reconstructive
and linear baselines, so no single predictive coordinate carries the wake and the
information is spread across many. The dimension at which each family first reaches a
wake $R^2$ of one half makes the same point from the other side: the predictive and
the wake-headed reconstructive states need eight latent dimensions
($\MinDJepa$ each), while the linear basis never reaches it at any dimension we
tested. The headline dimension of thirty-two sits on a plateau rather than a cliff,
the wake readability moving only $\PlateauSpread$ across sixteen, thirty-two and
sixty-four ($\PlateauWakeDThirtyTwo$ at thirty-two), so the choice of dimension is a
robustness statement and not a tuned operating point.

The parametric manifold coheres with the release parameters where the training data
resolve them. An impact-frame probe of the predictive latent recovers the gust
strength and the core diameter well ($\ProbeGJepa$ and $\ProbeDJepa$ on test\_b),
which is the atlas of figure~\ref{fig:atlas} coloured by parameter. The wall-normal
offset, unreadable at the linear-probe level in earlier work, becomes weakly readable
here ($\ProbeYJepa$ linearly and $\ProbeYkrrJepa$ with a nonlinear probe), plausibly
because the additional cases sample it more densely near the training centre; we note
this rather than lean on it, since the offset remains the least resolved parameter
(\S\ref{sec:flow_observables}).

A spatially resolved latent decodes the vorticity field more sharply than this
coefficient state, but it is of far higher dimension than the wall measurement and
so is not recoverable from sparse pressure. That decodability-versus-estimability
trade is the reason the coefficient state, not the field-resolved latent, is the
object this paper estimates; we take it up in \S\ref{sec:disc_decode} and quantify
its cost in Appendix~\ref{app:suppfigs}.
