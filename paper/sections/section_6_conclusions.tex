We asked what it takes to build, compress, advance and recover a reduced state
of an extreme vortex-gust encounter from wall pressure alone, and the answer
has four parts with a mechanism each. Building: under a predictive objective a
scalar lift anchor is what keeps the latent alive, a physics-constructed
near-body force-element head is the one conditioning that sharpens the peak
loads on top of it (peak-region $R^2$ of \TcPeakRTwoCLN{} across three seeds),
and the wake head carries the wake state; the anchor claim is about latent
health, the accuracy claim about a pre-registered increment, and they fail
independently. Compressing: lift information is dimension-invariant and
linearly accessible at four coefficients while the wake state needs sixteen,
so the honest compression statement is two-tier, compact and linear for the

load, distributed for the wake. Advancing: a \DirectFC{}
stays usable over forty steps where autoregressive rollout
compounds, and an oracle for the true gust parameters makes the forecast
worse, so the deployable route to the encounter is model plus pressure, not
parameters. Estimating: a pressure-only ensemble filter family sensing eight
taps tracks the load through the encounter phase by phase and in physical
units, an impact-phase analysis $R^2$ of \PoneTwoStageCovImpact{} at a
root-mean-square error of \PoneTwoStageCovRmse{} lift units in distribution
and \PoneTwoStageCovImpactC{} at the strongest held-out gusts with no
divergences, delivered by a knob-free \TwoStageKF{} rather than by noise
inflation; the innovation-consistency route to a larger noise band is refuted
on held-out training encounters, which separates what was calibration at
impact, now fixed by the state-dependent noise, from what is model error in
relaxation, where a fixed-lag smoother at a quarter convective time of latency
is the working answer. Across the design axis the predictive states are
uniformly wall-estimatable, where the reconstructive geometry fails
unpredictably with dimension and with training seed for a verified
observation-side reason, and this robustness, not any single cell, is the
deployment argument. Three facts bound the result: the in-distribution impact
median of the best filter does not beat a \StaticInv, whose
case fails instead in the tails and at the extrapolation boundary; the wake
enstrophy remains beyond any wall-limited filter of this flow, a sensing-
geometry bound rather than a state deficiency; and the mid-plane
representation reaches a three-dimensional observability boundary at the
highest gust strength. What the paper establishes is a lift-anchored,
wall-estimable reduced state, the supervision that produces it, and a filter
family whose failure modes are each attributed to calibration, to model error
or to sensing geometry rather than left unexplained.
