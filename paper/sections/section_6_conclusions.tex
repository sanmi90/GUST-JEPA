% Conclusions. Short, in the idiom of the reference papers' "Concluding remarks".

We asked which reduced-order state best carries the wake that governs the transient
load in parametric vortex-gust airfoil interactions, and reached a dynamical answer
with a geometric mechanism: a wake-supervised predictive latent builds a state from
which the wake is linearly decodable, whose rollout forecasts the wake and stays on
the encoded manifold, where a reconstruction objective carrying the same supervision
lets its rollout drift off the manifold along directions of negligible encoded
variance. We tested this by comparing a predictive
latent (JEPA, which takes no gust parameters), a reconstructive observable-augmented
autoencoder, and a proper orthogonal decomposition basis as reduced states at
$\vRe = 5000$, under a protocol in which a single probe family was held fixed so
that only the encoder varied. The instantaneous readability is the wake
supervision's: on the fixed test set the wake-supervised predictive latent reads the
wake (representational wake-enstrophy $R^2 = \NumReprWakeJepaTf$) where the
established no-wake baselines are at or below zero, but an objective-free supervised
encoder reads it as well, and under case-disjoint retraining a matched reconstructive
control reads it comparably (held-out median $\NumCvFullReconMedian$ against
$\NumCvFullJepaMedian$), so we do not attribute the readability to the predictive
objective. What the predictive objective robustly adds is dynamical: it reads the
wake consistently across retrains, forecasts it from the rollout where the
supervision-only and reconstructive states do not, and keeps the rollout on the
manifold, a property shared with the anti-collapse supervised latent and absent from
the unregularised reconstructive one. The same separation appears coordinate-free in
the rollout drift and in the topology of the encoded no-gust limit cycle. The latent
also carries the flow's own clocks: its spectrum recovers the shedding frequency on
an orbit whose modulus sits just above one, the wake response scales with gust
strength, and the gust resets the shedding phase. The same predictive state is the
most recoverable of the three from sparse wall pressure, so it is both wake-readable
and the most wall-recoverable of the three, the property a downstream estimator
would exploit. Three facts bound the result: the wall-normal offset is weakly
resolved, the mid-plane representation reaches a three-dimensional observability
boundary at $|G| = 4$, and the rollout error still exceeds the tolerance a
closed-loop control demonstration would require.
