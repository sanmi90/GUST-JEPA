Extreme vortex-gust encounters of a post-stall airfoil are wake-reorganisation
events: the load spike is the surface signature of leading-edge-vortex roll-up and
shedding, and the release parameters alone do not fix the wake after impact. Using
direct numerical simulations of a NACA~0012 airfoil at $\alpha = 14^\circ$ and
$\vRe = 5000$, perturbed by Taylor vortices spanning gust strength, core diameter
and wall-normal offset, we ask which reduced state preserves that wake. At fixed
dimension, under one shared probe and predictor, we compare a wake-supervised
predictive state, the lift-augmented reconstructive autoencoder of the
Fukami-Taira lineage, and a proper orthogonal decomposition basis; matched
wake-supervised controls separate objective from supervision.
Probed from held-out fields sixteen frames after impact, the predictive state makes
the wake enstrophy linearly readable ($R^2 = \NumReprWakeJepaTf$; case-level spread
shown) where the reconstructive and linear states are at or below the mean
predictor. The matched controls separate readability from dynamics: an
objective-free supervised encoder reads the wake equally well, so supervision
supplies the readability, while the predictive objective preserves it under rollout
where reconstruction suppresses it. The mechanism is geometric: the predictive rollout
stays on the encoded manifold and its no-gust shedding orbit remains a single
persistent loop, whereas the reconstructive rollout departs along directions of
negligible encoded variance and its no-gust loop fragments. Sparse wall pressure
recovers the predictive state best under a strictly pre-impact window; a validated
wake forecast and a closed-loop controller remain for future work.
