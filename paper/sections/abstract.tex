Extreme vortex-gust encounters of a post-stall airfoil are wake-reorganisation
events: the transient load is the surface signature of leading-edge-vortex
roll-up and shedding, and the release parameters alone do not fix the wake once
the gust has passed. A reduced-order model of such an encounter is useful in
deployment only if its state can be recovered from the sensors actually
available, the wall pressure, and advanced without leaving the region on which
that recovery was calibrated. Using direct numerical simulations of a NACA~0012
airfoil at $\alpha = 14^\circ$ and $\vRe = 5000$, perturbed by Taylor vortices
spanning gust strength, core diameter and wall-normal offset, we ask which
reduced coefficient state a sequential wall-pressure estimator can track through
the encounter. At fixed dimension, under one shared probe and predictor, we
compare a wake-supervised predictive joint-embedding state, a reconstructive
autoencoder in the Fukami-Taira lineage and a
proper orthogonal decomposition basis, with matched controls separating
objective from supervision. We find a division of labour: the supervision
supplies the wake's instantaneous readability and an anisotropic latent geometry
the forward rollout respects, while the multi-step predictive objective supplies
long-horizon rollout stability. A leakage-free ensemble Kalman
filter sensing eight wall-pressure taps tracks the lift (analysis $R^2$ of $\VfiltCLGOne$ to $\VfiltCLGFour$ for gust ratios of unity
and above) where every static inverse fails by a gust ratio of three; run
identically on each family, the filter tracks the load through any nonlinear
latent, and the predictive state is distinguished at the
strongest gusts, in calibration, and in carrying a wake-readable state. One tap
read over a thirty-frame window recovers the state as well as eight taps at an
instant, a trade read through the delay-embedding theorem, and the envelope
closes where the strengthening gust drives the interaction three-dimensional.
The filter is under-dispersed at the strongest gusts, where a calibrated noise
model, rather than more sensors, is the outstanding requirement.
