% Abstract. Single paragraph, <=250 words, held-out evidence first, ends on
% observability + forecastability (not model-based control). Reports the
% DNS/SOD2D provenance; the gain is attributed to the predictive objective
% trained with wake supervision (per the matched-architecture controls).

We ask whether a predictive latent objective yields a reduced-order state for
parametric vortex-gust airfoil interactions that forecasts better than a
reconstructive one. The data are our own direct numerical simulations (SOD2D) of
a NACA~0012 at $\alpha = 14^\circ$ and $\vRe = 5000$, perturbed by a discrete
Taylor vortex parametrised by gust strength $G$, core diameter $D$, and
wall-normal offset $Y$, across $84$ cases with held-out in-distribution and
$|G| = 4$ extrapolation tests. Three encoders are compared at matched latent
dimension, a joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA), a reconstructive
autoencoder after Fukami \& Taira, and a POD basis, under one shared predictor,
conditioning on $(G,D,Y)$, and probe family, so that only the encoder varies. We
judge each state not by reconstruction but by \emph{forward physical closure} of
six observables, sixteen frames after gust impact. The predictive latent leads on
the wake observables: on held-out encounters its representational wake-enstrophy
$R^2$ is positive ($0.75$) where the reconstructive and linear baselines are
negative, and a per-encounter paired test favours it on the wake error for most
held-out encounters. The mechanism is latent drift: under rollout the
reconstructive latent leaves its training manifold by an order of magnitude while
the predictive and linear latents stay within it. A conditioning-only floor and
matched-architecture, matched-supervision controls attribute the gain to the
predictive objective trained with wake supervision, not the architecture. The
same predictive state is also the most recoverable from sparse wall pressure, so
it is observable as well as forecastable. The world-model framing is motivation,
not a claim: a direct interventional test does not hold.
