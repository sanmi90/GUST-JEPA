% Abstract. Single paragraph, <=250 words, held-out evidence first, ends on
% observability + forecastability (not model-based control). Reports the
% DNS/SOD2D provenance; the gain is attributed to the predictive objective
% trained with wake supervision (per the matched-architecture controls).

We ask whether a predictive latent objective yields a reduced-order state for
parametric vortex-gust airfoil interactions that forecasts forward in time
better than a reconstructive one. The data are our own direct numerical
simulations (SOD2D) of a NACA~0012 at $\alpha = 14^\circ$ and $\vRe = 5000$,
perturbed by a discrete Taylor vortex parametrised by gust strength $G$, core
diameter $D$, and wall-normal offset $Y$, across $84$ cases with held-out
in-distribution and $|G| = 4$ extrapolation tests. Three encoders are compared
at matched latent dimension, a joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA), a
reconstructive autoencoder after Fukami \& Taira, and a proper orthogonal
decomposition basis, under one shared predictor, conditioning on $(G,D,Y)$, and
probe family, so that only the encoder varies. We judge each state not by
reconstruction but by \emph{forward physical closure} of six observables, sixteen
frames after gust impact. The predictive latent leads on the wake observables: on
held-out encounters its representational wake-enstrophy $R^2$ is positive
($0.75$) where the reconstructive and linear baselines are negative, and a
per-encounter paired test gives it the smaller wake-enstrophy error on $31$ of
$42$ encounters representationally and $27$ of $42$ under forecast. The mechanism
is latent drift: under rollout the reconstructive latent leaves its training
manifold by an order of magnitude while the predictive and linear latents stay
within it. A conditioning-only floor and a matched-architecture,
matched-supervision $2 \times 2$ control attribute the gain to the predictive
objective trained with wake supervision, not to the architecture. The same
predictive state is also the most recoverable of the three from sparse wall
pressure, so it is observable as well as forecastable.
