Extreme vortex-gust encounters of a post-stall airfoil are wake-reorganisation
events: the transient load is the surface signature of leading-edge-vortex
roll-up and shedding, and the release parameters alone do not fix the wake once
the gust has passed. We treat the encounter as a state-estimation problem, and
ask what it takes to build, compress, advance and recover a reduced coefficient
state from the only sensors a wing carries, its wall pressure. Using direct
numerical simulations of a NACA~0012 airfoil at $\alpha = 14^\circ$ and
$\vRe = 5000$, perturbed by Taylor vortices spanning gust strength, core
diameter and offset, a supervision cube over three aerodynamic heads shows that
a scalar lift anchor is load-bearing for predictive latent health, a
physics-constructed near-body force-element head sharpens the peak loads on top
of it (peak-region $R^2$ of \TcPeakRTwoCLN{} over three seeds), and a wake head
carries the wake state. Lift information is dimension-invariant down to four
coefficients, where it is linearly accessible; the wake state is not
compressible below sixteen. A direct multi-horizon quantile forecaster advances
the state where autoregressive rollout compounds, and knowing the true gust
parameters degrades the forecast. A leakage-free ensemble filter family sensing
eight taps then tracks the loads through the encounter, phase-resolved and in
physical units: a knob-free two-stage filter reaches an impact-phase analysis
$R^2$ of \PoneTwoStageCovImpact{} in distribution and \PoneTwoStageCovImpactC{}
at the strongest held-out gusts with no divergences, innovation-consistency
calibration of the forecast-derived noise is refuted in favour of a model-error
reading, and the predictive states remain uniformly estimatable across the
dimension axis where the reconstructive geometry fails unpredictably with
dimension and training seed. The wake enstrophy stays beyond any wall-limited
filter of this flow, a bound set by the sensing geometry rather than the state.
