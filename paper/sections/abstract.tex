An extreme vortex gust drives a post-stall airfoil through a transient
reorganisation of its wake, and the release parameters alone do not
determine the flow the vortex leaves behind. Nonlinear autoencoders compress
such encounters onto compact manifolds, but reconstruction-trained latents
evolve irregularly and forecast poorly, whereas linear
proper-orthogonal-decomposition latents forecast well yet reach no wake
observable at low dimension. We ask whether a predictive training objective
can instead yield a nonlinear state that is compact, forecastable and
estimable from the wall. Under this objective an observable anchor is
structural: a scalar lift target prevents the latent from collapsing where a
field observable alone does not, and a wake target loads the state with the
wake. At matched dimension and supervision, readability is supplied by the
supervision for predictive and reconstructive encoders alike, while the
multi-step predictive objective supplies rollout stability and the more
forecastable lift. Load information stays linearly accessible at four
coefficients, whereas the wake is a distributed code requiring at least
sixteen. A sequential ensemble filter reading eight wall-pressure taps tracks
the lift through the encounter, at impact-phase $R^2 =
\PoneTwoStageCovImpact$ in distribution and $\PoneTwoStageCovImpactC$ at the
held-out $|G| = 4$ boundary, uniformly across dimension and seed where the
reconstruction geometry is not. The wake enstrophy itself is not recovered
from the wall, a limit of the sensing geometry rather than the state. The
comparison gives a design rule for wall-estimable reduced states of extreme
aerodynamic flows.
