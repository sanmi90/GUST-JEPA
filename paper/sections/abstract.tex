Extreme vortex-gust encounters challenge reduced-order models because the transient
load is governed by wake reorganisation as well as by integrated forces. We ask which
reduced state faithfully keeps the wake that reconstruction discards and that remains
recoverable from sensors. Using direct numerical simulations of a NACA~0012 airfoil
at $\alpha = 14^\circ$ and $\vRe = 5000$, perturbed by Taylor vortices parametrised
by gust strength $G$, core diameter $D$, and wall-normal offset $Y$, we compare three
latent states at matched dimension: a fully unconditioned joint-embedding predictive
architecture, in which no gust parameters enter the encoder or the predictor, a
reconstructive autoencoder in the Fukami-Taira lineage, and a POD basis. All are
evaluated with the same probes, using the held-out closure of six observables.
The predictive latent encodes wake information that the baselines lose: probed
directly from the held-out field, its representational wake-enstrophy $R^2$ is $0.71$,
essentially the $0.75$ of a model that does receive the gust parameters, while the
reconstructive and linear baselines are at $0.06$ and below. The latent is single
cycle and its metric stays aligned with the flow's transport geometry in a
trajectory-local sense, whereas the reconstructive rollout leaves its latent manifold
and fragments the encounter topology. Rolled forward, the unconditioned predictor
tracks the wake through the encounter, which we show but do not present as a validated
forecast. Matched controls show that the advantage requires the predictive objective
together with wake-observable supervision, not the ViT architecture alone, and that
removing the gust parameters costs almost nothing in what the latent encodes. The
resulting state is also the most recoverable from sparse wall pressure, making it
observable as well as a faithful carrier of the wake.
