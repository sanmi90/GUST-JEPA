% (transport-consistent latent metric), anchors the wake claim on representation +
% mechanism with the case-clustered paired CI (Session 26 Track 1), demotes the
% forecast to consistent confirmation, ends on observability (positive). The

We ask what geometric property a reduced-order state needs to forecast
parametric vortex-gust airfoil interactions, and which encoder objectives confer
it; the action-conditioned world-model view is motivation only, since a direct
interventional test does not hold. The
data are our own direct numerical simulations (SOD2D) of a NACA~0012 at
$\alpha = 14^\circ$ and $\vRe = 5000$, perturbed by a discrete Taylor vortex
parametrised by gust strength $G$, core diameter $D$, and wall-normal offset $Y$,
across $84$ cases with held-out in-distribution and $|G| = 4$ extrapolation tests.
We compare three encoders at matched latent dimension, a joint-embedding
predictive architecture (JEPA), a reconstructive autoencoder after Fukami \&
Taira, and a POD basis, under one shared predictor conditioned on $(G,D,Y)$ and one
probe family, judging each by forward physical closure of six observables
sixteen frames after impact, not by reconstruction. The predictive
latent carries the wake structure the others do not: its representational
wake-enstrophy $R^2$ is positive ($0.75$) where the baselines are negative, and the
per-encounter improvement survives case-level clustering and a family-wide
correction (paired mean $+43$, case-clustered $95\%$ interval $[13, 77]$). The
mechanism is geometric: a reconstruction objective leaves the latent metric free, so
its rollout drifts off the data manifold, fragments the topology, and leaves the
optimal-transport geometry, whereas a predictive objective regularised against
collapse keeps each step transport-consistent and stays on-manifold. Matched
controls attribute the advantage to the predictive objective with wake supervision. The same
predictive state is the most recoverable of the three from sparse wall pressure, so
it is observable as well as forecastable.
