Extreme vortex-gust encounters of a post-stall airfoil are wake-reorganisation
events: the load spike is the surface signature of leading-edge-vortex roll-up,
shedding and recovery, and the release parameters alone do not fix the wake after
impact. Using direct numerical simulations of a NACA~0012 airfoil at
$\alpha = 14^\circ$ and $\vRe = 5000$, perturbed by Taylor vortices spanning gust
strength, core diameter and wall-normal offset, we ask which reduced state
preserves that wake. At fixed latent dimension and under one shared probe and
latent predictor, we compare a wake-supervised predictive state, a matched
wake-supervised reconstructive state, and a proper orthogonal decomposition basis.
Probed from held-out fields sixteen frames after impact, the predictive state makes
the wake enstrophy linearly readable ($R^2 = \NumReprWakeJepaTf$, with case-level
spread reported explicitly) where the reconstructive and linear states are at or
below the mean predictor. Matched controls separate readability from dynamics: an
objective-free encoder carrying only the wake supervision reads the wake as well,
so the supervision supplies the instantaneous readability, while the predictive
objective preserves it under rollout and the reconstructive objective suppresses it
under the same supervision. The mechanism is geometric: the predictive rollout
stays on the encoded manifold and its no-gust shedding orbit remains a single
persistent loop, whereas the reconstructive rollout departs along directions of
negligible encoded variance and its no-gust loop fragments. Sparse wall pressure
recovers the predictive state best under a strictly pre-impact window; we do not
claim a validated wake forecast or a closed-loop controller.
